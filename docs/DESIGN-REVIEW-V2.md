## What is working well

- The first five seconds succeed: at every reviewed viewport the name, Senior Software Engineer role, focus areas, 20+ years, stack summary and a primary experience CTA are immediately clear. At 320px the photo follows the identity content instead of displacing it.
- The editorial paper/ink/terracotta system, real photograph and restrained borders retain a differentiated, trustworthy identity. The photo crop is natural on mobile and desktop; it supports rather than overwhelms the content.
- The page hierarchy is professional and easy to scan. The Home gives a useful summary, while CV, About, Lab and case studies add depth without reproducing the same paragraph.
- Project status language is visually and semantically honest. `PREVIEW` and `LOCAL_DEMO` are distinguishable and the project pages make the hub boundary explicit.
- The mobile menu is compact, exposes the intended destinations, and provides 47.5px-high primary navigation targets. The skip link and focus outline are visible; no “accessible mode” has accidentally become the normal visual scale.
- No clipping, overlap, horizontal overflow, oversized mobile header, giant hero, broken image, or materially inconsistent card treatment was observed in the rendered matrix.

## Findings

### DR-V2-01 — Seven-step workflows become too narrow on wide desktop

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Evidence:** Home at 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1920×1080. In `Legacy → Modern`, the single-row layout confines seven steps to narrow vertical cards despite the centered max-width container; labels and descriptions wrap into many short lines and step 08 drops into an isolated second row. The dark `Software Engineering + AI` workflow and `How I Work` use the same seven-card density and become less scannable than their 1024px 4+3 counterparts.
- **Why it matters:** This is a central piece of the product story. The layout communicates a cramped card catalogue rather than a clear, deliberate engineering flow precisely at recruiter-facing desktop widths.
- **Recommended correction:** Keep the readable 4+3 / 4+4 grid at desktop widths until there is enough actual container width for a single row with a meaningful minimum card width (roughly 150–170px), or use a horizontal flow treatment with visible progression. Do not rely on viewport width alone when the content container is capped.
- **Acceptance criterion:** At 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1920×1080 each step title and supporting copy reads as a compact card rather than a word-by-word column; no orphaned final step; sequence remains immediately legible and retains the existing visual language.
- **Re-review:** **RESOLVED.** Re-rendered at 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1920×1080. The AI and How-I-Work workflows now use a balanced 4+3 layout; Legacy uses a legible 4+4 grid. Step 08 is no longer orphaned and all card copy scans naturally.

### DR-V2-02 — Muted microcopy does not meet the requested AA contrast baseline

- **Severity:** Medium
- **Evidence:** Rendered Home and project cards at all reviewed viewports. Computed text colors on the paper background include `#9A9590` (header secondary label, footer metadata and other muted labels; contrast 2.87:1 against `#FDFBF7`) and `#C07A5A` (`LOCAL_DEMO` and related 10px microcopy; contrast 3.29:1). These are below AA for normal text and especially problematic at the small rendered sizes.
- **Why it matters:** The status labels and supplementary navigation information are part of the content, not decorative texture; they need to remain readable for low-vision users and at normal mobile viewing distance.
- **Recommended correction:** Darken the relevant semantic text tokens (or increase size/weight only where hierarchy still permits) to achieve at least 4.5:1 on their actual paper surface. Preserve the terracotta/brass identity rather than replacing it with a generic dark gray.
- **Acceptance criterion:** All non-decorative text, including `LOCAL_DEMO`/`PREVIEW`-style status labels, header subtitle, footer metadata and muted helper copy, achieves 4.5:1 or better against its rendered background; visual review confirms the contrast remains refined rather than heavy.
- **Re-review:** **RESOLVED.** The rendered paper-background contrast is now 5.84:1 for muted header/footer text, 6.19:1 for `LOCAL_DEMO`, 7.07:1 for green `PREVIEW`, and 6.15:1 for brass `PREVIEW`. The darker tokens preserve the editorial palette without making the ancillary information visually heavy.
